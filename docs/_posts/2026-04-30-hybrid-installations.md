Between February and April 2026, BLUESHORES implemented its first experimental eco-engineering units across the project’s study areas in Ireland, the Venice Lagoon and the Eastern Scheldt in the Netherlands.

These installations translate the project’s modular hybrid blue-green infrastructure concept into field experiments. They combine natural and locally available materials, including wood and oyster shells, with biodegradable BESE elements. These components are intended to work together to reduce wave exposure, retain sediment and support saltmarsh establishment.

A common design concept is adapted to the environmental characteristics, tidal dynamics, ecological conditions and local requirements of each site. During the project we will monitor how hybrid interventions perform across contrasting coastal environments.

Installation activities involve local communities and people with practical knowledge of saltmarsh environments, bringing traditional experience and scientific expertise together. The experimental units provide a foundation for subsequent design refinement, helping BLUESHORES assess the potential for wider application of low-impact coastal protection solutions.
