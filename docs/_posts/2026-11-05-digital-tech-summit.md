At the Digital Tech Summit 2026, held in Copenhagen, Denmark, on 4–5 November, the Technical University of Denmark (DTU) showcased advanced marine robotic and mapping technologies that will support the research activities of BLUESHORES.

Understanding the complex interactions between coastal infrastructure, hydrodynamics and natural habitats requires detailed information about the geometry of the shoreline, the seabed and the individual eco-engineering elements.

BLUESHORES will combine high-resolution LiDAR and sonar mapping to generate detailed three-dimensional representations of its hybrid blue-green infrastructure interventions, capturing features both above and below the water surface.

![High-resolution three-dimensional mapping]({{ "/assets/img/posts/digital-tech-summit-3d-mapping.png" | relative_url }})

*Example of high-resolution three-dimensional mapping, illustrating the level of geometric detail that can be obtained through advanced sensing technologies.*

We plan to use these technologies to provide:

- High-resolution characterisation of foreshore morphology and experimental installations.
- Three-dimensional reconstruction of coastal infrastructure and surrounding habitats.
- Detection of morphological changes associated with erosion, sediment accumulation and habitat development.
- Detailed geometric information to support the development and refinement of hydrodynamic models.
- Better understanding of the interactions between waves, currents and hybrid coastal protection structures.
